# Brand Visual Anti-Patterns

Use this list to prevent common `design/brand-visual` mistakes that create inconsistency, provenance risk, or unusable deliverables.

## 1) Copying brand marks without context or constraints
**Pattern:** Reproducing logos or identity elements from memory/reference without stated brand rules.

**Why it fails:** Outputs may drift from approved geometry, spacing, or lockups and become unreliable references.

**Better approach:** Use provided brand specifications when available and label speculative stand-ins clearly.

## 2) Assuming asset or font licensing status
**Pattern:** Treating downloaded visuals/fonts as production-safe by default.

**Why it fails:** Teams cannot determine whether assets are replaceable, restricted, or approved.

**Better approach:** Record source class and approval status for each external asset; escalate unknown provenance as unresolved.

## 3) Inconsistent visual systems across deliverables
**Pattern:** Each output uses different typography logic, color mood, icon style, or spacing rhythm.

**Why it fails:** Brand recognition weakens and campaign cohesion collapses.

**Better approach:** Define a core system once, then adapt proportionally per format.

## 4) Overdecorated “premium” direction
**Pattern:** Heavy gradients, effects, metallic textures, and ornate type applied simultaneously to signal luxury.

**Why it fails:** The composition feels noisy and undermines trust.

**Better approach:** Express premium quality through hierarchy, whitespace, material realism, and controlled detail.

## 5) Vague style prompts with no traceability
**Pattern:** Directions like “make it luxury,” “make it modern,” or “more bold” without measurable constraints.

**Why it fails:** Revisions become subjective and cannot be replayed reliably.

**Better approach:** Translate style terms into specific palette, type scale, composition, and texture decisions.

## 6) Ignoring destination format and production context
**Pattern:** Delivering one generic visual for all channels without adapting size, crop, safe areas, or resolution.

**Why it fails:** Outputs break in placement and require last-minute redesign.

**Better approach:** Provide channel-specific specs and exports tied to intended usage.

## 7) Flattening files when editable sources are required
**Pattern:** Only final raster exports are provided for workflows that need layered edits.

**Why it fails:** Downstream teams cannot localize text, adjust hierarchy, or swap assets efficiently.

**Better approach:** Pair presentation exports with editable masters when workflow requires iteration.

## 8) Mixing stand-in and production assets without labeling
**Pattern:** Temporary images or generated marks appear indistinguishable from approved production elements.

**Why it fails:** Stakeholders may publish non-final assets inadvertently.

**Better approach:** Tag stand-ins clearly in filenames and handoff notes, with replacement guidance.

## Anti-pattern rejection checklist
- [ ] No uncontextualized brand mark replication.
- [ ] No licensing or provenance assumptions.
- [ ] No drifting typography/color system across outputs.
- [ ] No ornament-heavy premium styling.
- [ ] No vague style directions without measurable constraints.
- [ ] No format-agnostic exports for multi-channel usage.
- [ ] No missing editable sources when iteration is expected.
- [ ] No unlabeled stand-in assets in delivery sets.
